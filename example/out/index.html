The whole station

Four parts, no grid, no fuel

Sunlight becomes electricity in the array, the controller feeds it to the motor, and the pump lifts water from the well into the tank.

From the tank the water reaches the palms by gravity, even after the sun has set.

Station 1 · The source

The solar array

Each module turns about a fifth of the sunlight that falls on it into direct current. The tables are tilted so that the noon sun meets them squarely.

The power is never constant: it follows the sun, the dust and every passing cloud.

Station 2 · The brain

The controller matches the pump to the sun

It holds the array at its maximum power point and turns the direct current into alternating current for the motor.

When the light fades, it slows the motor down instead of stopping it, so water keeps flowing from early morning to late afternoon.

1 → 2

The centrifugal pump

The motor spins the impeller, the vanes throw the water outward, and the volute turns that speed into pressure.

The useful power is P = ρ·g·Q·H: the flow rate times the height it is lifted.

2 → 3

The tank is the battery

Instead of storing electricity, the station stores water at a height. A full tank holds the day's work as potential energy.

It costs less than a battery bank, lasts for decades and needs almost no maintenance.

Station 4 · The purpose

Water at the root, drop by drop

Gravity pushes the stored water through the drip line, and an emitter at each basin releases it slowly beside the trunk.

Drip irrigation loses far less water to evaporation than flooding the whole field.

Five parts on one shaft

Pulled apart along the shaft

The volute, the impeller, the bearing frame, the coupling and the motor share a single axis, so the unit comes apart and goes back together in one direction.

The only part that touches the water and turns is the impeller.
